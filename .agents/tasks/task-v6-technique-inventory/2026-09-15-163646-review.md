## High-level view

The change is deliberately additive and self-documents that intent: the inventory section states up front that it records WHICH techniques exist before the grammar normalizes HOW they are written, and the grammar section carries a demonstration table proving each of the 16 techniques still has a canonical spelling. This ordering is the core requirement and it holds — capture first, normalize second, label modes third.

All 16 techniques are accounted for with the four required elements each. The six COMMON items form a spine both modes share; the ten RARE items are called out as the fragile ones most likely to be lost in a cleanup pass, which is exactly why they are enumerated. Mode labels are attached per technique, and the three MODE C narrative devices (#10 AI second voice, #11 glitch degradation, #12 surveillance interlude) are labeled MODE C both at the technique level and again in the labeled-modes section.

The `[Tag Vocabulary]` contradiction is resolved cleanly in three places that used to pull in different directions: the canonical rules say don't ship it, the old converged-format passage is retitled "(early experiment / superseded)" with its vocabulary block marked NOT recommended, and the RESULTS LOG row for it now notes it was later dropped. There is no lingering passage that still recommends the block as current practice.

Preservation is honored throughout. The RESULTS LOG, Resolved/Working Model list, and Still Open list are all extended rather than gutted — eight new untested-technique rows, four new open questions — and the Style=WHAT / lyrics-field=HOW division-of-labor rule is intact and consistent across both files. STYLE_LIBRARY.md is linked, not duplicated, and does not appear in the diff at all.

The one real observation is cosmetic consistency: technique #14's inventory example demonstrates panning with the parenthetical/bare form the grammar's migration guidance steers away from, while its canonical-form row uses the namespaced spelling. Not a correctness problem, but a reader comparing the two could be briefly confused about which form is endorsed.

## Fragile techniques the normalization has to protect

The "lose nothing" guarantee is discharged row-by-row by the demonstration table, not just asserted. Two techniques carry real risk of being mangled by a naive normalization, and both are protected explicitly. Technique #13 (numeric BPM change) is kept distinct from the `[Feel: Half-Time]` perception shift, and the demonstration table restates that distinction so a cleanup pass doesn't collapse `[Tempo: 72 to 60 BPM]` into a feel tag. Technique #16 (in-line em-dash for breath) is called out as the one place a literal dash belongs, because the technique *is* the dash and a punctuation-normalization pass would otherwise destroy it. These are the sharpest parts of the change.

Within the RARE grouping where omission risk concentrates, the near-duplicate techniques are held apart rather than merged: #9 (call-and-response as narrative reveal) is distinguished from the plain #4, and #8 (voice-as-instrument muting) from ordinary instrument exit. #10 additionally carries a "must NOT be flattened into generic harmony" warning, which is the behavioral reason the mode is labeled at all.

## Migration-table resolutions worth noting

The six dialects each map to a canonical form, and two mappings do more than rename: (d) compact-pipe resolves to *delete the vocabulary block from output entirely* rather than rewrite it, and (e) per-line-prefixed collapses to a single section-top tag. Every observed dialect has a landing spot, so the recorded-but-not-endorsed observations are not a dead-end list.

## `[Tag Vocabulary]` contradiction resolution

The prior doc simultaneously described the vocabulary block as an emergent format part and (in CONFIRMED FINDINGS) as not-sung. The change closes that in four coordinated spots: canonical rule 3 says never ship it, the converged-format section is retitled "(early experiment / superseded)" with Part 1 labeled NOT RECOMMENDED and a "Drop it" instruction, the compact-pipe form is annotated as dialect (d) with "do not ship it," and the RESULTS LOG row notes it was later dropped. No surviving passage recommends the block as current practice.
