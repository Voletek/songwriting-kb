# Three in the Morning -- Song Brief

> Status: Approved  |  Target: Standalone single
>
> This is a CONCEPTUAL plan produced before any lyrics. It contains no verse lyrics. It documents
> the decisions the song is built from. The finished song lives next to this file at
> `Three_In_The_Morning.md`. Sample brief produced per `core/methodology/song-planning.md` and
> `.kiro/sops/09-planning-a-song.md`.

## 1. Song Thesis
A man at rock bottom walks the 3am streets because he cannot face his empty apartment, sees himself
in every broken person he passes, and confesses through desperate pleading that he does not know if
he will survive the night.

## 2. Semantic Analysis
- Subject: a sleepless man walking a city at 3am, unable to go home
- Characters: the narrator; a man on a bench with a needle; a woman yelling at a wall; two kids with a crack pipe; an old man on a corner who offers a cigarette
- Setting: nocturnal city streets, gutter level, roughly 3:00 in the morning
- Objects: streetlight, cigarette, curb, piss-stained concrete, corner store
- Actions: walking without stopping, witnessing the broken, receiving a stranger's kindness, pleading

## 3. Emotional Analysis
- Central affect: desperate nocturnal vulnerability (not generic "sad" -- a man coming apart)
- BRECVEMA mechanisms (2-3): emotional contagion (raw imperfect vocal transmits distress), episodic memory (3am as a felt scene), musical expectancy (unresolved minor harmony denies release)
- Journey (start -> through -> arrive): starts at compulsive physical desperation -> moves through horrified self-recognition in the gutter -> arrives at confessional prayer to a city that "won't refuse me"

## 4. Narrative Arc + The Turn
- Arc type: confession -> panic -> release (a prayer, not observation)
- Where the Turn is / what the bridge reveals: the Turn is the bridge. It reveals WHY he is on the street -- he broke things, she was right to lock the door, and he is terrified of who he was and who he is now. The single word "Please" lands alone as the most vulnerable moment. The final chorus recontextualizes the same words from confession into prayer. (Bridge-turn craft: see `core/methodology/songwriting.md` Step 14.)

## 5. Voice / Accent
- Register: male low register (approx D3-A4)
- Dialect / accent: slight East Tennessee Appalachian lilt -- soft twang on vowels, dropped g's, unhurried mountain pacing; a man from the hills who ended up in the city, NOT exaggerated country
- Emotional state (start vs end): starts weary and dissociated (talking to himself) -> ends cracked open and pleading

## 6. Genre Mapping
- Primary genre (70%): dark trip-hop (90s Bristol lineage)
- Texture genre (30%): cinematic strings / ambient electronic
- Fabbri 5-rules check: honors formal/technical (sub-bass, breakbeat, Rhodes), semiotic (nocturnal paranoia), and behavioural (headphone late-night listening) rules of trip-hop. (Fabbri's 5 rules defined in `core/methodology/songwriting.md` Step 7.)
- Instrument palette: deep sub-bass pulse, distant Rhodes piano, vinyl-crackle breakbeat barely there, cinematic strings, sparse electronic textures, reverb/delay as spatial instruments. (Palette selection: `references/SUNO_STYLE_GENRE_REFERENCE.md`.)

## 7. Key + BPM + Time Signature
- Key ( reasoning ): D minor -- dark and melancholic, sits naturally in the male low register, minor mode matches nocturnal desperation (mode = #1 emotional cue; see Key Selection in `SONGWRITING_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md`)
- BPM ( reasoning ): 66 BPM / Adagio -- slow enough to feel like a man barely moving, allows conversational delivery with weight on every syllable (see BPM-Emotion in `SONGWRITING_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md`)
- Time signature ( reasoning ): 4/4 -- trip-hop half-time feel; straight, barely-there pulse

## 8. Arrangement / Dynamics Sketch
- Growth pattern: additive / bloom -- from vinyl crackle and a single Rhodes note out to wide (never bright) sub-bass and strings, then stripped back for the bridge
- Negative space: the intro and the bridge (Rhodes and breath only) -- the quiet is the content
- Contrast pairs: sparse/wide, human breath/electronic cold, whispered verses/strained chorus
- Hook placement: hook signal early on the first sung vocal (aim within the first 5-30s of singing; genre-dependent -- see `core/methodology/songwriting.md` Step 8)

## 9. Structure Map + V6 Mode Label
- Section sequence: intro -> V1 -> V1 cont -> V2 -> V2 cont -> build -> pre-chorus -> chorus -> V3 -> V3 cont -> diminuendo -> bridge -> bridge cont -> chorus -> outro
- V6 mode label: Default/Roots -- a raw, human, roots-leaning vocal-forward track rather than concept-album cinematic material (mode label defined in `references/V6_TAG_DISCOVERY.md`)

## 10. Hook Approach
- Hook type: title / emotional-state hook
- Candidate hook line (conceptual target): "Three in the morning" as both temporal anchor and confession framework, paired with a plain repeatable statement of the emotional state (target phrase only -- not finished chorus lyrics)

## 11. Production / Commercial
- Target audience: late-night listeners of dark, atmospheric, confessional music
- Target length: ~4:30 (roughly 270s)
- Platform: streaming (headphone-first listening)
- Solo / duet: solo male vocal
- Standalone / album ( + position ): standalone single (no album continuity pre-check required)
