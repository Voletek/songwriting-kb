# Songwriting Craft — Power Steering

## Core Method

Write the chorus FIRST (Nashville method). Every song is a built structure. Prosody is law -- stressed syllables on the strong beats of the meter (square 4/4: usually beats 1 and 3; backbeat/syncopated styles push stresses onto anticipations or 2 and 4; 3/4 and 6/8: the downbeat of each group; pickups carry unstressed syllables before the bar). Show don't tell -- imagery over abstraction. The turn changes everything -- a bridge often works best when it creates contrast or a turn. [Tier 2]

## The Hook Test

A hook must be:
- Memorable after ONE listen
- Singable a cappella
- Textable to a friend
- Hook signal within ~5-30 s of the first SUNG word [Tier 2] (genre-dependent; the full chorus typically lands by ~45-60 s of track time in pitch-ready commercial songs — a separate metric)
- The emotional peak of the chorus

## Lyric Rules

- Target 7-12 syllables per line [Tier 2]; 13-15 = review stress density and singability at tempo; 16+ = Compression Test (or justify as intentional)
- [Tier 2] No universal rhyme-scheme default: ABCB (XAXA) is a common conversational option (less closure); pick per section -- stable/closed for choruses, open for pre-choruses/bridges
- Strongest rhyme that doesn't bend meaning or syntax (Pattison ladder: perfect > family > additive/subtractive > assonance > consonance) -- perfect/family for closure, looser for motion; never force syntax. Detail: `references/RHYME_AND_LYRIC_CRAFT.md`
- [Tier 2] V2 usually adds new information or angle (unless reiteration is part of form design)
- No clichés unless subverted
- Imagery = physical, sensory, specific

## The Turn

Every great song has a moment where meaning flips. The bridge often works best when it reveals something new or creates contrast. [Tier 2] The final chorus hits different because of what the bridge said. Design for this.

## Academic Foundations (Critic Uses These)

- **Moore's 4 Layers:** A wide range of popular recordings can be analyzed through explicit beat, functional bass, harmonic filler, melodic layer -- generalized descriptions, not prescriptive rules (not all present in every example)
- **Tagg's Musemes:** Every timbre/riff carries cultural meaning — ensure sounds SIGNIFY correctly
- **Lacasse's Proxemics:** Vocal distance (intimate→public) must match emotional content and CHANGE over the song
- **Moylan's Soundbox:** The mix is a 3D space (stereo, depth, frequency) — design it intentionally
- **Dodge et al. (2025) — Scope of Vision:** Ambition matters — a daring song that partially fails > a safe song that fully succeeds
- **Skip Test:** If any 15-second window has no new information, contrast, or reward — it's a dead zone
