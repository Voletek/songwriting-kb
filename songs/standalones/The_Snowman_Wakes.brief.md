# The Snowman Wakes -- Song Brief

> Status: Draft  |  Target: Standalone (seasonal single, companion to Violent Night)
> Working title; alternatives in section 12. Bed: **Storms of the North, take A** (`790fd603-c25f-45c9-9e42-fdc0cf2e741a`,
> workspace "Nordic Folk(Instumentals)"), measured Nov 24, 2026.

## 1. Song Thesis
The children thought they built a snowman; they woke what was sleeping in the snow.

## 2. Semantic Analysis
- Subject: A Frosty-like snowman story turned monstrous: the snowman is a frost giant (a Norse rime-giant) that was frozen under the hill, and the children's snow is its body.
- Characters: The frost giant (singer, first person in the chorus); the village children who built it; the villagers (heard, not seen).
- Setting: A northern village on the edge of a frozen hill, one winter night.
- Objects: A stolen hat and scarf (the "magic hat" nod, here an offering that wakes it), coal eyes, a corn-cob pipe swapped for an ox bone, footprints the size of doorways, the ice on the fjord.
- Actions: Building, waking, the first step, walking into the village, the village hiding, the rampage, the thaw / return to the snow, the promise to come back with the next snow.

## 3. Emotional Analysis
- Central affect: Gleeful dread: the fun of a monster story, with real menace.
- BRECVEMA mechanisms (2-3): Brain-stem reflex (sudden stomps, the climax); rhythmic entrainment (the stomping pulse); evaluative conditioning (a children's-carol innocence twisted).
- Journey (start -> through -> arrive): Innocence (children playing) -> unease (it moves) -> terror (it walks into town) -> awe (it is enormous and ancient) -> a cold promise (it will come back).

## 4. Narrative Arc + The Turn
- Arc type: Escalation with a cyclical ending (the threat returns every winter).
- The Turn (bridge, over the bed's lull ~1:35-2:05): the point of view flips. Until now we watched it; now it speaks to the listener, quietly: it remembers being here before the village, and the children didn't make it, they woke it.

## 5. Voice / Accent
- Register: Very low, huge-sounding bass-baritone; the giant should feel bigger than the room.
- Dialect / accent: A slight old-Norse / Scandinavian weight (rolled r's), not a cartoon.
- Emotional state (start vs end): Slow, sleepy, cold in Verse 1 -> commanding chant in the choruses -> close, almost tender menace in the bridge -> a low promise at the end.
- Candidate saved voice: **Kael IronHammer** (deep gravelly bass-baritone with a Nordic burr) is the closest fit. To be confirmed before rendering (SOP 10).

## 6. Genre Mapping
- Primary genre (70%): Nordic battle folk (the bed).
- Texture genre (30%): Dark children's carol / nursery chant (the Frosty nod: a simple sing-along hook turned sinister).
- Fabbri genre-rule types that matter most: Semiotic (Nordic instruments = the old North, the giant's world) and behavioural (chant chorus, crowd-sized).
- Instrument palette: From the bed: nyckelharpa, bowed lyre, Nordic flutes, tagelharpa drones, frame and hand drums, wooden stomps, bone rattles.

## 7. Key + BPM + Time Signature
- Key: C minor (measured on the bed; a cover may move it).
- BPM: ~145 (measured 145.5).
- Time signature: 4/4 by the beat tracker, heard as a rolling 6/8 by the listening check. Confirm by ear; either works for a stomp.

## 8. Arrangement / Dynamics Sketch
- Growth pattern: Follows the bed: creeping, rising, a lull, then the rampage, then a fade.
- Negative space: The opening ~0:16 and the bridge lull are sparse: the giant's breathing and the wind.
- Contrast pairs: Children's sing-song vs the giant's chant; whisper (bridge) vs roar (final chorus).
- Hook placement: First chorus near 1:05 (where the bed first opens up).

## 9. Structure Map + V6 Mode Label
- Section sequence (fitted to the bed's measured sections):
  - Intro 0:00-0:16: instrumental, wind, a distant stomp
  - Verse 1 ~0:16-0:56: the children build it (sing-song, almost a nursery rhyme), then it opens its eyes
  - Pre-Chorus ~0:56-1:08: the first step; the ground shakes
  - Chorus 1 ~1:08-1:35: the giant's chant (hook)
  - Bridge ~1:35-2:05: the Turn, close and quiet over the lull
  - Final Chorus ~2:05-2:43: the rampage; biggest moment
  - Outro ~2:43-3:07: the thaw, the giant sinks back into the snow, a whispered promise
- Rhyme & stability plan per section:
  - Verse 1: AABB couplets (nursery-rhyme closure, deliberately sweet)
  - Pre-Chorus: unrhymed or open (instability as it moves)
  - Chorus: closed XAXA chant with a repeated title line
  - Bridge: ABCB, loose (the voice turns intimate)
  - Outro: one closed couplet, the promise
- V6 mode label: Default/Roots with MODE C touches (spoken/whispered bridge and outro).

## 10. Hook Approach
- Hook type: Title / chant hook with a rhythmic stomp motif.
- Candidate hook line (conceptual target): a three-beat stomp phrase ("thump, thump, thump" turned into the giant's footsteps), resolving on the title "the snowman wakes". The Frosty nod lives in the stomp and the hat, not in borrowed lyrics.

## 11. Production / Commercial
- Target audience: Dark-holiday listeners; fans of Violent Night; Nordic folk and fantasy audiences.
- Target length: `Length Target: radio, intro <= 35s` (3:00-3:45; the creeping intro is intended and runs past the 15 s default, so the intro limit is overridden).
